Building Stronger Foundations: Redesigning Intern Recruitment & Onboarding

A project nobody asked me to do. I noticed colleagues struggling with a broken onboarding process, and used a diary study and service design to fix it from the inside.

Role
Self-initiated Service Designer
Organization
International Rescue Committee (NY/NJ)
Duration
Fall 2023
Methods
Two-part diary study, stakeholder analysis, journey mapping, personas
Context

A problem I found, not one I was assigned

While working as Community Relations Lead at the International Rescue Committee's NY/NJ office, I kept noticing the same friction surface in different conversations: managers and interns talking past each other during recruitment and onboarding, with no one person owning the process end to end. Nothing in my job description asked me to fix this. But it was exactly the kind of "insight I couldn't just leave on the table" that pulled me toward design in the first place, so I initiated the project myself.

My Role & Approach

A two-part diary study, end to end

I spearheaded and executed a two-part diary study to trace the entire intern lifecycle — from first contact during recruitment through the first weeks of onboarding — capturing both the manager's and the intern's side of the same process in near-real time, rather than relying on retrospective interviews alone.

I paired the diary data with semi-structured interviews, a stakeholder analysis, journey maps, and personas to represent the different goals in play: what a hiring manager needed to feel confident handing off responsibility, and what a new intern needed to feel oriented instead of thrown in.

Key Findings

The gap lived in the handoffs, not the steps

The recruitment and onboarding steps mostly existed — what was missing was ownership of the transitions between them. Communication broke down specifically at the handoff points between recruitment and onboarding, and between a manager's expectations and what an intern was actually told to expect. No single document or person carried context across that boundary.

Outcome & Impact

Redesigning the handoffs, not just the paperwork

I synthesized the findings into recommendations that redesigned the recruitment-to-onboarding handoff and clarified manager-intern communication at each stage, then presented the insights to the relevant teams. The redesigned process shipped in time for the Summer 2024 intern cycle.

Reflection

This was the first time I built something instead of just writing up what I'd found — the moment the anthropology-to-design shift stopped being theoretical. Noticing a human problem wasn't the finish line anymore; I wanted to sit with it long enough to hand back something that actually changed how people experienced the process.
