Design

Evaluating & Redesigning the Usability of a Gamified EdTech Solution

Heuristic evaluation and usability research on a math-learning platform for middle schoolers, uncovering the information-hierarchy problems that were quietly working against its own gamification.

Role
User Experience Consultant
Organization
dfusion Inc.
Duration
October 2024 – January 2025 (8 weeks)
Methods
Heuristic & usability evaluation, interactive design, competitor research

Challenge

Conducted heuristic evaluations, usability testing, and semi-structured interviews to uncover user pain points and inefficiencies in the flow of an EdTech platform designed to gamify math learning for middle school students.

Finding

Identified issues related to misleading information hierarchy, limited visibility of system status, and high cognitive load.

Impact

Designed wireframes, mockups, and interactive prototypes for core dashboards and student-facing interfaces, now implemented across 10 school districts nationwide.

Background

A science-driven innovation company

dfusion Inc. is a science-driven innovation company specializing in behavioral health, prevention, and digital health innovations. They use evidence-based, interactive technology, such as mobile apps and game-based learning tools, to address and reduce social inequities and improve health outcomes.

Project Context

Joining the Fantasy Sports Math League team

I joined dfusion as a User Experience Consultant, contributing to the launch of Fantasy Sports Math League (FSML), an interactive web-based game that supports math learning through fantasy sports. FSML is designed for a wide range of learning environments, including classrooms, distance learning, after-school programs, homeschool networks, and youth organizations. The game uses real professional players and current statistics to create dynamic and engaging math experiences.

Key contributions (8-week timeline)

  • Conducted experience audits and usability evaluations, applying usability heuristics and human factors principles to a gamified learning solution, and identified key user pain points.
  • Designed wireframes, mockups, and interactive prototypes for the core team drafting page, now launched across 10 school districts nationwide.
  • Initiated competitor research to identify trends and gaps in supplementary educational curricula, engaging with local public school districts to understand classroom needs and inform product development.
User Research

No standardized UX guidelines to design against

Problem statement. FSML currently lacks standardized UX guidelines, resulting in screen inconsistencies and inefficiencies during the design-to-development handoff. A redesign is essential to ensure a cohesive experience that keeps students motivated and engaged in learning math.

Target user. FSML targets middle school math learners and serves as a supplementary curriculum adaptable to classrooms, distance learning, after-school programs, homeschool networks, and youth organizations.

Stakeholder & user interview

To understand the goals and existing pain points of the current version of the game, I conducted five semi-structured interviews with the product manager to clarify the vision of FSML.

Additionally, I interviewed five middle school students who had previously used various gamified learning solutions to gain insights into their math learning experiences and pain points with similar tools.

Heuristic Evaluation

Four violations, one usability audit

I conducted a heuristic evaluation of the entire FSML website to identify usability problems.

Visual hierarchy

The current design contains instances where there is no clear visual hierarchy, leaving users confused about the goal and hindering them from completing tasks.

Visibility of system status

Pages lack communication with the user, leaving them unaware of the system's current state or the outcome of their previous interactions.

Recognition vs. recall

Multiple pages require users to remember information from previous screens instead of providing guidance, which increases their cognitive load.

Error prevention

The current design is prone to slips, which negatively affect learning outcomes.

Redesign Rationale

Four fixes, one redesigned page

The redesign of the FSML Team Drafting page focused on four main concerns identified through heuristic evaluation and usability testing.

Visual hierarchy

The redesign breaks the main Team Drafting Page into three control panels. The first column, Available Players, allows users to view all available players and customize their search by filtering out unwanted qualifications.

The second column, My Team, shows the user's current roster, including the number of players by position. The third column, My Statistics, enables users to calculate their expenditures on drafting and compare them against the total budget.

Visibility of system status

The redesign allows users to see their team composition at any point during the drafting process. In user interviews, students expressed that they often felt lost when there was too much information on the page and were unsure whether their actions were completed correctly.

The redesign addresses this by providing users with active status updates. For example, in the Team Composition section of the My Statistics panel, each helmet represents a player the user has selected. At a glance, users can see how many players they have and how many more they need to draft to complete their team.

Recognition vs. recall

The previous design assumed users were familiar with American football and required students to know player names and their qualifications.

The current design introduces detailed filtering options that allow students to sort players by bye week, rank, team, position, or cost. This way, students no longer need to memorize player names but can instead make informed choices and customize selections based on their needs.

Error prevention

One key value raised by the product team during stakeholder interviews was that the game should be challenging yet supported with resources. FSML is not just a regular sports game, it also needs to reinforce math skills related to algebra.

In the current design, I added a player info pop-up window for each player. This feature allows students to click on a player to view their cost and ranking compared to others. As a result, students can make informed decisions when drafting their team rather than selecting players at random.

Interactive Prototype

Testing the flow, not just the screens

Beyond static mockups, this redesign was also built out as a live, interactive Figma prototype, so the drafting flow could be experienced end to end rather than judged screen by screen.

Product Launch

Live across 10 school districts

The redesigned Team Drafting page shipped and is now live across 10 school districts nationwide.
