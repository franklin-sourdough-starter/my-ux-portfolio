My Journey

How I learned to research, then build.

I've never studied design in a straight line. I got here by way of a camera, then a field notebook, and only then a Figma file, and I think that path is exactly why I design the way I do.

01
2019 – 2020 · Emerson College · Coursework in Filmmaking

Why I wanted to make documentaries

I studied filmmaking first because I wanted to be inside a story rich enough that an audience anywhere in the world could feel something true through it. Documentary, specifically: I wasn't drawn to invented plots, I was drawn to real people's lives, told well enough to travel across cultures and still land.

What I found in the coursework, though, was a program built around equipment and technical execution (camera settings, rigs, editing pipelines) long before it got to the people in front of the lens. I didn't transfer out because I stopped caring about story. I transferred because I wanted to get to the human material faster, and with more depth than a film crew's access usually allows.

02
2021 – 2023 · NYU · B.A. Anthropology

Learning to live the question, not just ask it

Anthropology gave me what filmmaking's technical track couldn't: a direct route into how humans are shaped by their environment. Ethnographic method asks you to do something a camera crew rarely has time for: to live alongside the people you're trying to understand, long enough that their ordinary becomes visible to you.

My honors thesis grew out of that instinct. I spent a semester in qualitative interviews with international Chinese students, exploring what I came to call their "Ultra-Flexible Citizenship": the constant, quiet self-censorship they practiced online to protect their lives back home while studying under an entirely different set of norms abroad. Sitting with twenty different versions of that same negotiation taught me something I still rely on: the most important insight is rarely the one a participant says first. It's the one that surfaces once they stop performing an answer and start describing a life.

"This research endeavor trained me to be a systems thinker, a deeper understanding of how sociopolitical factors can influence the way people learn, adapt, and protect themselves."
03
2024 – Present · SJSU · M.S. Human Factors & Ergonomics (UX Concentration)

Insight was never the finish line for me

By the time I graduated, I loved gathering insight and untangling a complicated human problem, but I kept running into the edge of what anthropology alone could do with it. Fieldwork could tell an institution exactly where it was failing people. It couldn't hand them the redesigned form, the clearer workflow, or the onboarding flow that fixed it.

That gap is why I moved into human-centered design. I wanted the making process back, the part of filmmaking I'd loved from the start, but this time in service of the same rigor I'd learned in the field. Now, across internships at Kroger, Apple, and an EdTech startup, and a fellowship with a school district in Delaware, my process looks the same every time: get close enough to real behavior to trust the insight, then stay in the work long enough to ship something that meets both the user's need and the business's.

I'm still, in a way, making documentaries. I just changed the medium: from film, to field notes, to interfaces people actually have to use.

How the Three Fields Show Up in My Work

What I actually carry from each chapter

From Filmmaking

An instinct for narrative: I don't just report findings, I frame them so the people who need to act on them actually feel the stakes. Every case study, readout, and slide is built to move an audience, not just inform one.

From Anthropology

Ethnography, semi-structured interviewing, contextual inquiry, diary studies, thematic and qualitative coding in MAXQDA, survey design, and a working discipline of cultural relativity. I don't assume my read on a behavior is the only valid one.

From Human-Centered Design

Journey mapping, personas, heuristic evaluation, usability testing, wireframing and prototyping in Figma, stakeholder facilitation, and research operations: the craft that turns an insight into something a team can actually build.
