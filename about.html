My Journey

How I learned to research, then build.

I didn't take a straight path into design. I started with a camera, then moved into fieldwork, and only later ended up in Figma. That path is a big part of why I approach design the way I do.

01
2019 – 2020 · Emerson College · Coursework in Filmmaking

Why I wanted to make documentaries

I studied filmmaking first because I wanted to tell stories that felt true to people no matter where they were from. Documentary, specifically: I was never that interested in invented plots. I wanted real people's lives, told well.

What I found in the coursework, though, was a program built around equipment and technical execution (camera settings, rigs, editing pipelines) long before it got to the people in front of the lens. I didn't transfer out because I stopped caring about story. I transferred because I wanted to get to the human material faster, and with more depth than a film crew's access usually allows.

Filmmaking taught me how to take an ordinary moment and tell it in a way that actually holds someone's attention, and that none of it matters if you don't finish the edit and get something in front of an audience.
02
2021 – 2023 · NYU · B.A. Anthropology

Learning to ask better questions

Anthropology gave me what filmmaking's technical track couldn't: a direct route into how people are shaped by their environment. Ethnographic method asks you to do something a camera crew rarely has time for: spend real time with the people you're trying to understand, long enough that their everyday life becomes visible to you.

My honors thesis grew out of that instinct. I spent a semester doing semi-structured interviews and diary studies with a group of international students about how they navigated living and studying under a different set of cultural norms than the ones they grew up with. That project taught me how to ask a question that gets past someone's rehearsed answer, and how to treat any single behavior as part of a much larger system rather than a one-off choice.

Anthropology trained me to ask better questions and to think in systems: to look at one person's choice and ask what larger set of factors actually produced it.
03
2024 – Present · SJSU · M.S. Human Factors & Ergonomics (UX Concentration)

Insight was never the finish line for me

By the time I graduated, I loved gathering insight and untangling a complicated human problem, but I kept running into the same wall: insight on its own doesn't change anything for the people living the problem. I wanted to be the one who actually ships the fix, not just the one who points it out.

That gap is why I moved into human-centered design. I wanted the making process back, the part of filmmaking I'd loved from the start, but this time in service of the same rigor I'd learned in the field. Now, across work spanning merchandising tech in food retail, consumer tech, and EdTech, my process looks the same every time: get close enough to real behavior to trust the insight, then work through discovery, making, and validating until what ships actually meets both the user's need and the business's.

In a lot of ways, I'm still doing the same work I started with. I just changed the medium: film, then field notes, now interfaces people actually have to use.

Human-centered design trained me to finish what research starts: to carry an insight through making and validating until it's a real product decision, not just a slide.
How the Three Fields Show Up in My Work

What I actually carry from each chapter

From Filmmaking

An instinct for narrative: I don't just report findings, I frame them so the people who need to act on them actually feel the stakes. Every case study, readout, and slide is built to move an audience, not just inform one.

From Anthropology

Ethnography, semi-structured interviewing, contextual inquiry, diary studies, thematic and qualitative coding in MAXQDA, survey design, and a working discipline of cultural relativity. I try not to assume I already understand why someone does what they do.

From Human-Centered Design

Journey mapping, personas, heuristic evaluation, usability testing, wireframing and prototyping in Figma, stakeholder management, and research operations: the craft that turns an insight into something a team can actually build.
